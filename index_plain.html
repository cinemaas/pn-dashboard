    ⚖️ CRITICAL — a rental booking is NOT filming rights
    Read this before anyone books a house.

    Booking a short-term rental gives you a licence to occupy as a guest. It does NOT convey commercial filming rights, set dressing, moving or removing furniture, crew/equipment loads, or use of the property's image. Nearly every vacation-rental manager's standard terms prohibit commercial use and events — booking a house and then filming in it is typically a breach, and the manager can shut you down mid-day.

    Always paper a SEPARATE location agreement, and make sure the OWNER signs it — not the manager. A rental manager's agency agreement usually does not give them authority to grant filming rights. Treat the manager as the door and the owner as the signature.

    Carry: production GL naming both owner and manager as additional insured, certificate delivered before load-in, documented pre/post condition walkthrough. Have the owner confirm nothing in their mortgage, homeowner's insurance, or HOA/condo docs bars commercial use — that's a real deal-killer that surfaces late.

    💰 The 31-day angle — worth money. MA has required STR registration + room-occupancy excise (plus local option and, in participating Cape towns, a Water Protection surcharge) since 2019. But stays of 31 days or more fall OUTSIDE the short-term-rental definition and are exempt from that excise. Our window is ~54 days — so a single continuous block booking is likely both cheaper and simpler than a string of short stays, and it sidesteps the STR regime entirely. ⚠ 2026 rates/thresholds NOT verified — have the production accountant confirm before this goes in the budget. (Registration/tax sits with the owner/operator, not with us as occupant.)

    Permits, rule of thumb: filming entirely on private property with zero public impact often needs no town film permit. The moment you touch the public way — street parking, basecamp, generators, cable across a sidewalk, intermittent traffic control — you need a permit and almost certainly MA police details, a real and frequently underestimated line item. ⚠ bylaws are town-by-town; none of our 15 towns were individually verified.

    🔑 Expired listings & the MACRIS×assessor play — verified against the actual MLS rules
    THE COMPLIANT ARCHITECTURE — this is the whole thing:
    Agent queries Pinergy → filters to EXP/CAN/WDN matching our criteria → does NOT export → makes the owner contacts themselves → brings us a curated shortlist with permission already softened. Same outcome, agent's licence stays clean, and we get a better list because a human filtered it for "would this owner actually say yes."

    Why you can't just be given the file (MLS PIN Rules, amended thru 3/25/2026, §8.0): data is for Participants/Subscribers "solely in their capacities as such" and "No Participant or Subscriber shall cause or permit any data… to be transmitted… to any individual or entity, other than… a Participant or Subscriber." Penalty is only $50–$1,000 — but it carries "possible suspension of service," i.e. the agent's livelihood. Never ask them to absorb that. The ones who'd say yes aren't the ones you want.
    Why it isn't on Zillow either: §10.3(c) lets public sites display only active, under-agreement and sold — expired/withdrawn/cancelled are deliberately excluded. That's exactly why the data has value.
    But the RULES restrict the COMPILATION, not the underlying FACTS. An agent saying "31 Crowell came off market in March, owner's motivated, I know the family, want me to call?" is professional conversation, not data redistribution.

    THE STATUS CODES — verbatim from MLS PIN Attachment B:
    · ★★★★★ EXP (Expired) — best target. No agency relationship remains, so you can approach the owner directly with zero interference issue. They tried to sell, failed, and are carrying a big house.
    · ★★★★ CAN (Cancelled) — listing terminated early. Often means the owner decided not to sell — which is fine, even good: they're staying, they're carrying it, and a location fee is found money.
    · ★★★ WDN (Withdrawn) — ⚠ the listing contract is STILL LIVE. Approaching the owner direct can tortiously interfere with the broker's agency. Route via the listing agent.
    · ★★★ BOM (Back on Market) — underrated: a deal just collapsed. Owner deflated, house already vacant + staged, timeline blown up. Very receptive to bridge income.
    · PCG (Price Change) — a string of PCGs is a tell: repeatedly cut and still unsold = highly motivated even while listed.
    · CSO (Coming Soon) — useless: rules bar showings entirely. SLD — don't dismiss; a house sold 6 months ago has a NEW owner carrying a big new mortgage on a big historic property.
    Ideal target profile: EXP or CAN · 4,000+ sqft · pre-1900 · high days-on-market with multiple PCGs before expiring · currently vacant. = an empty, camera-ready, expensively-carried house whose owner has already emotionally detached.

    ⚠ THE CANAL SPLITS THE MLS. MLS PIN covers Plymouth/Duxbury and all of the North Shore. Cape Cod & Islands MLS (CCIMLS) covers the Cape. Bourne and Sandwich straddle the line — so recruit a dual-member agent, or one agent per system, or you'll be blind on half the Sagamore hub.

    WHAT TO SAY — volunteer the constraint before they raise it. It signals you've done this and removes their main reason to decline:
    "I'm not asking for a list — I know you can't send one and I'm not putting you in that spot. Run the search on your side: off market in the last 18 months, [towns], 4,000+ sqft, pre-1900. You keep the results. Then pick the three or four where you think the owner would actually pick up, and you make the call — tell them a production wants to pay them to film there. If they're interested, introduce us. If not, no harm and you've had a warm conversation with a lead you'd have cold-called anyway. And if any of them decide the fee is nice but selling would be nicer — that's your listing." Their three wins, say them out loud: (1) a warm re-list lead — the industry stat is that 43% of expireds relist and sell with an agent; (2) real money to a client currently carrying an unsold house; (3) production credit + a relationship with a show that'll shoot the region again. Offer a per-property finder's fee for anything we actually shoot at, on top of the owner's fee and separate from any later commission.

    ⭐⭐ THE BEST PURE-PUBLIC-DATA PLAY — MACRIS × ASSESSOR. No MLS, no gatekeeper, no DNC exposure.

    THE KILLER FILTER: pre-1900 + 4,000+ sqft + high assessed value, then cross-reference the owner's MAILING address against the PROPERTY address.
